## Essence
Tending what's been placed in your hands.

## Light
Responsibility is the gardener's steady attention: watering, weeding, showing up for what depends on you. It's being someone others can count on, and owning your part when things go wrong. Plainly: keeping your commitments, caring for what's yours to care for, and answering for your choices. There is quiet dignity in being dependable.

## Shadow (excess)
Too much, and you start tending gardens that were never yours. Over-responsibility carries everyone's feelings and outcomes, leaving no room to be carried yourself. Care curdles into control, or exhaustion.

## Void (deficiency)
Without responsibility, life can feel unmoored, as if things just happen to you. Promises slip and trust thins. Sometimes this grows from having carried too much too young. Taking back small, chosen responsibilities can rebuild the ground.

## What keeps it whole
Trust lets responsibility rest. You don't have to hold everything; others are capable too. Sharing the load is itself a responsible act.

## Questionnaire notes
- What are you carrying that was never yours to carry?
- Where do you avoid owning your part, and what are you afraid it would mean?
