## Essence
A warm hand held out to suffering, your own included.

## Light
Compassion is the heart turning toward pain instead of away from it. Like a lantern carried into a dark room, it doesn't make the darkness vanish, but no one has to sit there alone. In plain terms: you notice when someone is hurting, and you let that move you to care, gently and without judgement. It begins at home, in the way you speak to yourself on hard days.

## Shadow (excess)
Too much, and the lantern-bearer forgets to rest. Compassion can tip into rescuing, fixing, or carrying other people's lives as if they were yours. You might care for everyone but yourself, and call the exhaustion love.

## Void (deficiency)
When compassion runs dry, the heart grows a hard shell. Others' pain starts to feel like an inconvenience, and your own like a weakness. Often the well hasn't gone dry, it's been overdrawn. Tending yourself is usually how it fills again.

## What keeps it whole
Boundaries are the lantern's glass: they let the flame shine without being blown out. Caring well includes knowing what is yours to carry and what is not.

## Questionnaire notes
- Who receives your gentlest voice, and who gets your harshest? Where do you land in that?
- Where does caring for others become a way of not tending to yourself?
